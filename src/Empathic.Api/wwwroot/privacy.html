Privacy notice

How the prototype currently handles information and what must change before a wider public launch.

Last updated: 8 September 2026.

The Empathic Movement website is an early-stage prototype. The current public pages do not use advertising trackers or sell personal information. The registry prototype can store creator and cultural-work information that an operator deliberately submits.

Information processed by the prototype

The registry can process creator display name, institution, country, roles, cultural-work metadata, external references and content-fingerprint source material. Avoid submitting confidential information, passwords, private keys, sensitive personal data or content you do not have permission to process.

Storage

The current application uses server-side JSON storage for prototype records. Production deployment should replace this with an appropriately secured data store, access controls, retention rules, backups and a documented lawful basis for any personal-data processing.

Blockchain and hashes

A cryptographic hash can be used to prove that data existed in a particular form without publishing the underlying content. Production deployments should avoid writing personal data directly to an immutable blockchain because deletion and correction rights can conflict with permanent ledgers.

Cookies and analytics

The current website does not require analytics cookies. If analytics, CRM, authentication or third-party services are added later, this notice and any consent controls should be updated before they are enabled.

Your choices

Do not submit personal or cultural material to the prototype unless you are authorised to do so. A production launch should publish a working contact route for access, correction, deletion and other applicable privacy requests.

Future production policy

This prototype notice is not a substitute for a jurisdiction-specific privacy policy. Before collecting community-member information at scale, the founders should complete a data map, retention schedule, processor review, privacy-risk assessment and legal review appropriate to the countries in which the platform operates.
